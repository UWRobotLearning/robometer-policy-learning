Reward models for
BC-initialized policy learning

Start with π₀.₅ or a small flow-matching policy. Choose what the reward model means, what supervision changes it, and how its signal improves the policy.

A mathematical design guide, not a report of completed runs. “Frozen RM,” “fine-tuned RM,” and “distilled RM” describe different operations. No learned dynamics or MPPI planner is required for the model-free recipes below.

Notation and the action-chunk clock

Let xt be the task instruction plus observation history available at time t. Robometer predicts progress pθ(xt) ∈ [0,1], and optionally success probability qθ(xt). θ₀ is the pretrained RM; φ₀ is the BC policy. They are separate parameter sets. Use history because a video RM need not be a single-image state function.

The policy samples an action chunk At. If k actions are actually executed, its discounted reward and bootstrap discount are:

Rt(k)=∑j=0k−1γjrt+j,Γt=γk,mt=1−dt.R_t^{(k)}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\gamma^j r_{t+j},\qquad \Gamma_t=\gamma^k,\qquad m_t=1-d_t.

dt marks a true terminal. A time-limit truncation only permits bootstrapping if the chosen task formulation continues beyond it. Use actual k on early termination; predicted chunk horizon and flow integration steps are different quantities. All equations below use physical-step γ.

No RM gradients

1 · Keep Robometer frozen

A. Use its output as a reward

rtRM=pθ0(xt+1)−1,J(ϕ)=Eπϕ ⁣[∑t≥0γtrtRM].r_t^{\rm RM}=p_{\theta_0}(x_{t+1})-1,\qquad J(\phi)=\mathbb E_{\pi_\phi}\!\left[\sum_{t\geq0}\gamma^t r_t^{\rm RM}\right].

This is the progress-minus-one convention in the local OGPO LIBERO adapter. Train a separate V or Q from these rewards and improve the policy. Robometer stays fixed; the policy can still learn. Subtracting one also affects duration incentives when termination times differ.

B. Use its output as a value heuristic

V~(x)=a pθ0(x)+b,A^t=Rt(k)+ΓtmtV~(xt+k)−V~(xt).\widetilde V(x)=a\,p_{\theta_0}(x)+b,\qquad \widehat A_t=R_t^{(k)}+\Gamma_t m_t\widetilde V(x_{t+k})-\widetilde V(x_t).

Use this TD residual as a policy-weighting heuristic. A progress score is not automatically Vπ: progress describes completion so far; value predicts future discounted reward under a particular policy. Fit or choose a,b on a calibration split in return units. An affine map is still only an approximation. Under a −1 step cost, the ideal value with n remaining steps is −(1−γn)/(1−γ), which is nonlinear in time.

With R = 0, this becomes a discounted progress-improvement score; it is not evidence of optimizing simulator success. With true rewards, longer Monte Carlo returns reduce reliance on the heuristic bootstrap. A state-only RM cannot rank unexecuted actions without observed successors, a learned dynamics model, or an action-conditioned Q.

C. Use it as potential-based shaping

rt′=rttask+β[γmtΦ(xt+1)−Φ(xt)],Φ=pθ0.r_t'=r_t^{\rm task}+\beta\bigl[\gamma m_t\Phi(x_{t+1})-\Phi(x_t)\bigr],\qquad \Phi=p_{\theta_0}.

Under a fixed potential, consistent discounting and zero terminal potential (or the equivalent absorbing-state treatment), the shaping sum telescopes to a policy-independent initial-state offset. This preserves the underlying task objective; adding raw progress each step generally does not. Pure shaping with zero base reward supplies no new terminal objective.

Optional: HuRL annealing
r~t=rttask+(1−λ)γmtV~(xt+1),γ~=λγ,λ↑1.\widetilde r_t=r_t^{\rm task}+(1-\lambda)\gamma m_t\widetilde V(x_{t+1}),\qquad \widetilde\gamma=\lambda\gamma,\qquad \lambda\uparrow1.

This jointly removes a value bonus and restores the original discount. Compare against a discount-only schedule to identify whether gains come from the RM or the shorter horizon. It changes the intermediate learning problem. See HuRL.

Adapt the reward predictor

2 · Fine-tune with the original RM objectives

“Original objective” means the supervised losses used to train the RM. It does not mean maximizing the RM’s own predictions. Collect BC-policy successes, failures and near-misses, then update heads or LoRA adapters with outcome/progress/preference supervision.

LRM=λpLprogress+λsLsuccess+λprefLpreference.\mathcal L_{\rm RM}=\lambda_p\mathcal L_{\rm progress}+\lambda_s\mathcal L_{\rm success}+\lambda_{\rm pref}\mathcal L_{\rm preference}.
Lprogress=E(x,y)∈Dp[−log⁡Pθ(b(y)∣x)],Lsuccess=E[BCE⁡(qθ(x),ys)].\mathcal L_{\rm progress}=\mathbb E_{(x,y)\in\mathcal D_p}[-\log P_\theta(b(y)\mid x)],\quad \mathcal L_{\rm success}=\mathbb E[\operatorname{BCE}(q_\theta(x),y_s)].
Lpreference=E[BCE⁡(σ(ℓθ(τA,τB,c)),yA≻B)].\mathcal L_{\rm preference}=\mathbb E[\operatorname{BCE}(\sigma(\ell_\theta(\tau^A,\tau^B,c)),y_{A\succ B})].

b(y) bins progress; soft-bin targets use cross-entropy against a target distribution. The local trainer also supports continuous regression. Robometer’s preference logit ℓ is produced from a joint comparison of two videos; it need not equal a difference of summed rewards. These equations abstract masking and sampling weights. See the Robometer objectives and its local trainer.

For a verified successful trajectory, normalized time to first completion can supply progress labels; do not force every failed trajectory to reach progress 1 at timeout. Success labels must reflect whether the visible frame has completed the task. Successful-versus-failed pairs supply preferences even without dense failure labels.

Self-training limit: regressing to a frozen teacher’s own outputs transfers its predictions. It cannot, by itself, teach the model that a confidently scored near-miss is a failure. Use independent success feedback, annotations, or comparisons for correction.

Alternate collection → supervised RM update → replay relabeling → policy update. Retain prior data and failure examples; use versioned labels and a held-out near-miss set to distinguish actual correction from overfitting.

Adapt a value predictor

3 · Fine-tune the RM into a value function

If “fine-tune according to the original reward” means learn returns under a frozen RM reward, this is a different objective. Fix r = pθ₀ − 1, initialize a value model from RM features or weights, and fit Bellman targets. Alternatively use simulator success as the fixed reward.

yt=Rt(k)+ΓtmtVθˉ(xt+k),LV=E[(Vθ(xt)−sg⁡(yt))2].y_t=R_t^{(k)}+\Gamma_t m_t V_{\bar\theta}(x_{t+k}),\qquad \mathcal L_V=\mathbb E[(V_\theta(x_t)-\operatorname{sg}(y_t))^2].

Here θ̄ is a lagged target and sg stops gradients through y. On-policy samples evaluate the rollout policy; arbitrary replay instead requires an explicitly chosen off-policy value-learning method. The output now estimates return, not progress. Replace or rescale a [0,1] progress head when returns live in a different range.

For action selection, learn Q(x,A) with a policy-evaluation target:

ytQ=Rt(k)+Γtmt EA′∼π[Qψˉ(xt+k,A′)].y_t^Q=R_t^{(k)}+\Gamma_t m_t\,\mathbb E_{A'\sim\pi}[Q_{\bar\psi}(x_{t+k},A')].

Keep the reward source identifiable: freeze θ₀ when it defines the target reward. Simultaneously moving that reward and its value predictor confounds reward adaptation with policy evaluation. A separate critic on policy features is often cheaper than updating the full RM for every TD batch.

Adapt the reward to policy failures

4 · Fine-tune with inverse-RL feedback

Initialize a reward critic fθ from the RM, then alternate a reward update against current-policy negatives and a policy improvement step:

min⁡θ  Ex∼d−[fθ(x)]−Ex∼d+[fθ(x)]+λregΩ(θ),max⁡ϕ  Eπϕ ⁣[∑tγtfθ(xt+1)].\min_\theta\;\mathbb E_{x\sim d^-}[f_\theta(x)]-\mathbb E_{x\sim d^+}[f_\theta(x)]+\lambda_{\rm reg}\Omega(\theta),\qquad \max_\phi\;\mathbb E_{\pi_\phi}\!\left[\sum_t\gamma^t f_\theta(x_{t+1})\right].

Demonstration versus policy occupancy gives the usual adversarial-IRL interpretation. Using only successful versus failed episodes instead gives an outcome-conditioned contrastive variant; it is not identical to matching full policy occupancy. Choose how frames and trajectories are weighted.

The rss27/irlhf snapshot’s rbmir recipe trains Robometer’s progress head with mean failure score minus mean success score, then distills its predictions into h. Its external patched trainer is required. The Ω term above is a design choice (for example, a suitable gradient penalty or prior regularizer), not a claim that this runner enables it. An unbounded critic needs scale control; a bounded progress head can instead saturate.

The branch’s irlhf arm is different: it keeps the base teacher frozen and uses r = f + 12h, with WGAN training only f. That is a persistent additive reward; it is not a one-time initialization. Both designs can feed a model-free flow policy, but neither requires an MPPI planner.

Optional representation and compute choice

5 · Distill into DINO or policy features

zt=E(ot),Ldistill=E[(hη(zt,c)−sg⁡(pθ(xt)))2].z_t=E(o_t),\qquad \mathcal L_{\rm distill}=\mathbb E[(h_\eta(z_t,c)-\operatorname{sg}(p_\theta(x_t)))^2].

Freeze E and the teacher for each student update; train η. Replace DINO with frozen PaliGemma / π₀.₅ features or the small policy’s encoder features. Include instruction c for multiple tasks. A value student should regress return targets; a progress student should regress progress. Adding action conditioning alone does not turn progress imitation into a Q-function.

MPAIL benefits because its planner scores many imagined latent transitions: a compact h(z′) is cheap and available inside that latent model. In model-free learning you can instead label real trajectories asynchronously and cache their rewards. Distillation becomes useful when RM inference or latency is a measured bottleneck.

A single-frame student cannot reproduce information that the teacher only sees in a video or in hindsight. Use a short history/recurrent student, or accept and measure that approximation. Hindsight labels are valid offline supervision, but must not be presented as causal online value estimates. Distillation also transfers teacher errors; it does not create new task feedback.

Same actor interface for every method

Use a BC policy or a small flow head

Begin at φ = φ₀. For a demonstrated or replay action chunk A, sample ε ∼ N(0,I), u ∼ Uniform[0,1], and use a noise-to-action path:

Au=(1−u)ϵ+uA,LBC=E ⁣[∥vϕ(Au,u∣o,c)−(A−ϵ)∥2].A_u=(1-u)\epsilon+uA,\qquad \mathcal L_{\rm BC}=\mathbb E\!\left[\|v_\phi(A_u,u\mid o,c)-(A-\epsilon)\|^2\right].

One simple policy-improvement recipe is advantage-weighted flow regression with an explicit BC anchor:

wt=min⁡(wmax⁡,exp⁡(sg⁡(A^t)/T)),Lπ=EDrollout[wtLFM,t]+α EDdemo[LFM,t].w_t=\min(w_{\max},\exp(\operatorname{sg}(\widehat A_t)/T)),\qquad \mathcal L_\pi=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D_{\rm rollout}}[w_t\mathcal L_{{\rm FM},t}]+\alpha\,\mathbb E_{\mathcal D_{\rm demo}}[\mathcal L_{{\rm FM},t}].

T is a weight temperature; α retains demonstration behavior. Advantages can come from the frozen heuristic above, Monte Carlo returns with a baseline, or a learned critic. Normalize/clip weights and stop gradients through them. This is an AWR-style flow-regression proposal, not an exact policy-gradient identity or a description of every OGPO run. A frozen state-only RM cannot provide ∂Q/∂A for direct action optimization.

Train only the action coordinates whose execution the advantage evaluates, or use an explicit full-horizon critic. If the policy predicts 50 actions but executes 4, assigning the 4-step advantage to all 50 introduces an attribution approximation.

π₀.₅: retain its pretrained conditioning and action expert; optionally freeze the VLM and tune action-expert LoRA. Keep its existing action normalization, padding mask, chunk convention, and flow-time direction. The equations here use noise at u=0; OpenPI’s implementation may use the reversed clock. No DINO encoder is required.
Small flow head: compute frozen image/language features plus proprioception, then BC-train a compact conditional velocity network. During RL, update the head with weighted flow loss or your existing actor-critic method. A separate critic and an optional RM student may share frozen features while retaining different objectives.
BC-initialized actor→Real rollouts + feedback→Score / adapt RM→Returns / advantages→Flow-policy update ↻

A BC model is the policy parameterization and initialization. Once it is improved using reward-derived weights or actor-critic gradients, the procedure is reinforcement learning. With no policy updates, collecting data and evaluating an RM alone will not improve the policy.
